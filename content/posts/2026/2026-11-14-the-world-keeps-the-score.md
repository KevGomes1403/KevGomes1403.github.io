_I wrote this back in May, having just emerged from a long, dark winter in upstate New York. As I enter into this next season of prolonged darkness and immense uncertainty in my personal life, I find these musings on authenticity, hard work, and discovering fulfillment especially poignant._

There I was at the edge of Cornell’s campus, immersed in the roar of the waterfall that flanks Rhodes Hall, my skin warm against the sunlight that seldom reached Ithaca during its dreary winter. I presented a poster for my Master’s thesis and had just learned that my work wasn’t picked for any of the award categories. I was crestfallen and pissed. I was so confident that both my contributions and the problems I worked on far surpassed what anyone else had done in the past year. Industry veterans and startup founders were lining up in my DMs for this same work but these department folks didn’t see the value.

Standing below the raging waters under a towering canopy that mushroomed in green only recently, I was hit with a realization. None of this matters. I was being shown that external accolades are worthless, that all the work I did in the literal darkness of the dreadful upstate New York winter will never be validated by anything beyond the precious innate satisfaction of a job well done. A cliched realization, yes, but one that the day’s events magically showed me. I was almost convinced that special forces in the universe coalesced to present me this teaching.

Actually, I’m pretty much convinced of that. Yes, the law of attachment is bullshit: you can’t _just_ manifest your way into achieving your goals. At the same time, it’s not. Jordan Peterson talks about how our perception _is_ our reality. If we’re aimless and playing video games all day or doomscrolling in every liminal moment we have, we are effectively simulating our life, as Arthur Brooks puts it. What we pay attention to creates the world we effectively operate in. The world presents to us what is most adjacent to our aims, and any obstacle to them are invitations to grow so we’re better equipped for the next leg of the journey.

Pick a shitty, unfulfilling path and we will only surround ourselves with people who anchor us down. Pick one that aims towards our highest calling and we’ll find ourselves around people who elevate us.

That’s why it’s essential for us to show up as our authentic selves. Some might argue it’s a moral duty for each of us to express ourselves fully as the flawed and one-of-a-kind human beings we are. Only when we express ourselves most organically do we attract the right kinds of people. Only when we share our work do we create chances for someone out there to be positively affected. And only when we put our hearts out into the world do we invite true love.

All to say that the world out there is rich and rewarding, and at the same time a reflection of us. It can show us when we’re truly aligned with our souls and when we might need some course corrections. So pay attention.
